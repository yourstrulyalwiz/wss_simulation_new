## 4. Reconcile intervention cash and physical benefits

Use a common intervention output structure separating:

- Additional collected revenue.
- Recurring operating savings.
- Additional recurring operating costs.
- Implementation capex.
- Physical service benefits.
- Unit-cost adjustments.

### Tariff and collection efficiency

Use consistent volume, tariff, and collection assumptions.

Calculate combined incremental collected revenue as:

DeltaRevenue[t]
= Q_scenario[t] × Tariff_scenario[t] × Collection_scenario[t]
- Q_BAU[t] × Tariff_BAU[t] × Collection_BAU[t].

Do not assume that the entire tariff increase is collected when collection efficiency is below 100%.

Individual contribution displays must reconcile with this combined result using a documented attribution order. Do not double-count interaction effects.

### NRW

Separate physical-loss recovery from commercial-loss recovery.

Allocate recovered physical water explicitly between:

- Additional service/sales.
- Reduced production.
- A user-defined combination.

Allocation shares must total 100%.

Only the portion used for reduced production generates avoided production costs. Only the portion used for additional service can support the corresponding physical service benefit.

Commercial-loss recovery improves billing of existing consumption; it does not create physical water.

Apply collection efficiency to additional billed revenue, and reconcile NRW-related revenue with the shared revenue calculation so it is not added twice.

Do not assume recovered water automatically creates new connections without any necessary connection or upgrade costs.

Keep NRW implementation capex and recurring maintenance costs separate.

### Custom interventions

Check and correct the following if present:

- “Both sectors” duplicates the full cash benefit in Water Supply and Sanitation.
- Switching sector tabs changes saved intervention assignments.
- “Safely managed only” cost reductions also affect basic-service costs.

A shared cash benefit must be counted once, using explicit sector allocations or a clearly defined shared pool.
