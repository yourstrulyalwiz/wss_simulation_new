## 1. Objective and scope

The tool should help users understand:

- What investment is required over time to achieve WSS coverage targets.
- How budget execution, operational efficiency, and capex efficiency affect financing and access gaps.
- How additional net utility cash can be reinvested directly or committed to servicing a loan that finances investment earlier.
- What additional public financing would still be required after these interventions.

Keep the tool as a forward-looking scenario model.

The final methodological decision is to EXCLUDE actual-delivery monitoring and catch-up replanning. Retain the simulated BAU and intervention coverage projections, but do not introduce actual-versus-plan reporting, implementation tracking workflows, or automatic rescheduling of missed investments.

Preserve existing geographical scopes, separate Water Supply and Sanitation inputs, historical data, target milestones, saved scenarios, and exports.

## 2. Correct investment requirements and financing-gap accounting first

Maintain two distinct calculation pathways:

A. Target investment pathway:
The infrastructure additions and replacement expenditure required each year to achieve the specified coverage milestones.

B. Resource-constrained scenario pathway:
The coverage that projected BAU or intervention resources can support.

Investment requirements must come from A. Simulated achieved coverage comes from B. Do not repeatedly add the difference between target coverage and simulated coverage to an asset stock.
